Professional Self-Assessment

Professional Background & Growth

My professional background is rooted in information technology, with experience spanning technical support, systems administration, enterprise resource planning (ERP), and technical training. Throughout my career, I have developed a strong interest in understanding how technology supports business operations and how improvements to systems and processes can benefit an organization.

I chose to pursue a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science because I wanted an education that would broaden my technical knowledge rather than limit me to a particular area of information technology. Although my primary professional interests are in IT infrastructure, systems, and administration rather than full-time software development, understanding programming, software architecture, algorithms, databases, and security makes me a more capable and versatile IT professional. These subjects have helped me better understand the systems I support and strengthened my ability to investigate problems, evaluate solutions, and communicate with technical teams.

Completing the Computer Science program has also reinforced the importance of approaching technical challenges with a structured and analytical mindset. Through coursework involving software development, testing, database management, and security, I have gained a greater appreciation for the decisions that influence system reliability, maintainability, and performance. Developing my ePortfolio has provided an opportunity to revisit earlier work, recognize areas of improvement, and demonstrate how my technical abilities have progressed throughout the program.

As I continue my career, I intend to apply this combination of practical IT experience and formal computer science education to increasingly complex technical and organizational challenges. My long-term goal is to advance into IT management and leadership, where a broad understanding of technology, business processes, and the people who depend on those systems will be particularly valuable.

Collaboration & Communication

Collaboration and communication have been important aspects of my professional development. In my current role as an ERP Systems Analyst, I work closely with the Manufacturing Resource Planning (MRP) Team, which consists of department leaders from across the organization. Together, we investigate potential issues within our ERP system, evaluate possible resolutions, and identify opportunities to improve existing processes. This collaborative approach helps ensure that technical decisions consider the operational needs of the departments affected by them.

These experiences have reinforced the importance of communicating technical information clearly and understanding different perspectives before implementing changes. A solution that appears technically sound may not adequately address the needs of the people who depend on the system. My Computer Science coursework has complemented this experience by strengthening my ability to analyze technical problems, explain proposed solutions, and communicate the reasoning behind design decisions.

My coursework also provided practical experience with software engineering, algorithms, data structures, databases, and security. Through projects involving application development, testing, and database integration, I gained a better understanding of how different components of a system interact and how design decisions affect performance, reliability, and maintainability. This knowledge is particularly valuable in my current role supporting enterprise systems, where identifying the underlying cause of an issue often requires understanding how multiple systems and processes work together.

The combination of technical education, professional experience, and leadership development has helped prepare me to approach IT challenges from both technical and organizational perspectives. Rather than focusing solely on implementing a solution, I have learned to consider its broader impact on users, business processes, and long-term system support.

ePortfolio Overview

My ePortfolio demonstrates the knowledge and skills I developed throughout the Computer Science program through three enhancements to an Android inventory management application originally created in CS 360: Mobile Architecture and Programming. I selected this application because it provided an opportunity to revisit an earlier project, evaluate its limitations, and apply the additional knowledge I gained throughout my coursework. Rather than developing three unrelated projects, I chose to improve the same application across software design and engineering, algorithms and data structures, and databases.

The first enhancement focused on software design and engineering by introducing the ability to edit existing inventory records, improving input validation, and integrating the new functionality into the application's existing structure. The second enhancement addressed algorithms and data structures by implementing inventory searching and sorting while preserving filtered results. The third enhancement focused on database design and security through schema improvements, database migration, and the implementation of salted PBKDF2 password hashing to better protect user credentials.

Together, these enhancements demonstrate my ability to evaluate an existing system, identify opportunities for improvement, implement changes, and validate their effectiveness. They also illustrate how software engineering, algorithms, databases, and security are interconnected rather than isolated areas of computer science. The accompanying code review, enhancement narratives, source code, and screenshots provide evidence of my technical development and the decisions made throughout the enhancement process.

Although my career interests remain focused on information technology and systems administration, the work presented in this portfolio demonstrates how my Computer Science education has expanded my ability to understand, evaluate, and improve the technologies that support modern organizations. These skills will continue to benefit me as I pursue greater technical responsibilities and future opportunities in IT leadership.

Algorithms and Data Structures

For this enhancement, I expanded the Android inventory management application with search and sorting functionality. Users can now search inventory records by item name and sort the displayed results to locate and organize inventory information more efficiently.

The completed enhancement demonstrates the application of algorithms and data structures while maintaining application state so that searching and sorting work together without resetting filtered results.

Databases

For this enhancement, I expanded the database design and security of the Android inventory management application. I introduced inventory categorization, updated the SQLite database schema, and implemented migration procedures to preserve existing inventory records and user accounts during application upgrades.

The completed enhancement demonstrates database design, data migration, testing, and secure credential management through the implementation of salted PBKDF2 password hashing. These improvements strengthened data integrity, security, and the application's ability to accommodate future changes.
